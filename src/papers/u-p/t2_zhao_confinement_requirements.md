# T2 Zhao Flexible Confinement Requirements

## Objective

This document translates Zhao et al.'s flexible confined boundary condition into implementation requirements for strict triaxial compression in the current u-pw SPH branch.

## Zhao Eq. 51

Zhao's mechanical confinement is introduced as an additional isotropic pressure contribution in the SPH momentum equation:

```text
a_i^conf = sum_j m_j * (sigma_c_i + sigma_c_j) / (rho_i rho_j) * grad_i W^R_ij
```

where:

- `sigma_c` is the imposed confining pressure magnitude;
- `W^R` is the renormalized kernel gradient in the paper;
- the term is evaluated through material neighbors, not dummy membrane particles.

For constant `sigma_c`, the term should vanish inside the body where the kernel support is complete. At a free or open boundary the support is truncated, so the cancellation fails in a controlled way and the remaining term acts as an inward traction.

## Sign Convention

The current branch uses positive `ConfiningStressP0` to mean external compression. This is compatible with the existing CPU stress-divergence sign convention, where the confining term is added as a positive isotropic stress-like pair contribution.

For implementation and diagnostics:

- positive lateral confinement should project inward in the radial direction;
- net force over a symmetric cylinder should be near zero except for cap/edge asymmetry and axial loading;
- the term should not be stored as pore pressure;
- unless an initial-stress route is explicitly enabled, the term should not overwrite the material stress tensor.

## Kernel Truncation Mechanism

The method depends on a clear distinction between complete and truncated kernel support:

- interior particle: neighbor support nearly closed, confining contribution cancels;
- lateral surface particle: support missing outside the cylinder, residual acceleration points inward;
- cap particle: support missing outside the cap, residual acceleration points axially unless excluded;
- edge/ring particle: support is truncated by both cap and lateral surfaces and can contaminate both directions.

Strict triaxial loading therefore cannot simply apply the confining term to every free surface. It must isolate the lateral membrane.

## Kernel Completeness Index

Zhao's large-deformation treatment identifies near-boundary particles with:

```text
f_i = sum_j (m_j / rho_j) W_ij
```

The paper reports empirical thresholds:

- 2D: about `f_i <= 0.55`;
- 3D: about `f_i <= 0.70`.

For this branch, the first step should be diagnostic only:

- compute `f_i`;
- plot and summarize its spatial distribution;
- count particles below `0.70`;
- compare those particles against geometric lateral/cap classifications;
- compare `f_i` with confining acceleration magnitude.

The threshold should not be treated as final calibration until we see it on the actual triaxial particle cloud.

## Large-Deformation Rescaling

Zhao also describes a rescaling of the confining contribution by an initial/current magnitude ratio `l0/ln` to compensate for changing particle separation when smoothing length is fixed.

T2 recommendation:

- do not implement rescaling first;
- diagnose whether confining acceleration weakens during deformation;
- only add rescaling after lateral selection and `f_i` diagnostics are stable.

The reason is simple: rescaling can mask a bad selector or rough particle cloud. It should be a second-stage correction, not the first source change.

## Initial Hydrostatic Stress

Zhao's circular verification shows cleaner equilibrium when the specimen starts with a hydrostatic stress state equal to the imposed confinement. Without that initial stress, sudden application of confinement launches stress waves and requires damping to equilibrate.

For strict triaxial reproduction, there are two separate concepts:

- mechanical boundary traction generated by `FlexibleConfiningStress`;
- initial in-situ/hydrostatic effective stress stored in the material stress tensor.

The current T1/T2 path has the first concept partially implemented, but not the second as a general XML-controlled triaxial stage.

## Damping And Ramp

If initial hydrostatic stress is not implemented immediately, the confinement should be ramped and damped:

- ramp `ConfiningStressP0` smoothly over a short isotropic stage;
- keep axial loading off during this stage;
- monitor velocity, net force, and stress-wave decay;
- only then start axial compression.

This is less strict than a hydrostatic initialization, but safer as the first T3 smoke.

## Cylinder Particle Layout

Zhao's triaxial setup uses a fan-shaped cylindrical particle lattice to reduce boundary roughness. This matters because the method obtains surface normal and effective area from kernel truncation. Rough cut-cell cylinders create noisy truncation, false cap/lateral classifications, and noisy radial acceleration.

For T3:

- keep the current simple scaffold for diagnostics if needed;
- but plan a smooth cylindrical particle cloud before any paper-level comparison;
- report radial roughness and `f_i` histograms as geometry quality metrics.

## Lateral-Only Selection Requirement

Strict triaxial compression has different boundary roles:

- lateral membrane: prescribed confining pressure;
- top platen: prescribed axial velocity/displacement and optional cap slip condition;
- bottom platen: fixed or controlled support;
- pore-pressure boundary: separate hydraulic decision.

Applying flexible confinement to top/bottom surfaces would corrupt the stress path. Therefore, T3 must support lateral-only selection before a strict triaxial smoke is considered meaningful.

## T2 Requirement Summary

Required before strict triaxial confinement:

1. `f_i` kernel-completeness diagnostic.
2. Lateral/cap geometric classification.
3. Radial projection diagnostics for confining acceleration.
4. Net force and COM acceleration diagnostics.
5. Ramped confinement smoke without axial loading.
6. Axial loading plus confinement smoke only after the confinement-only case behaves.
7. Initial hydrostatic stress strategy after the diagnostic route is stable.
8. GPU deferred until CPU validation.
