1   INTRODUCTION

Because of the porous nature of geomaterials, geotechnical engineering design commonly involves accounting for fluidsaturated soils, fluid flow, and the subsequent pore water induced alteration of soil behavior and deformation. The stability
of slopes, tunnels, foundations, excavations, or engineered earth structures can be susceptible to excess pore pressure
buildup or seepage forces. An extreme design case occurs whenever the application of load to saturated soil is conducted
under conditions inhibiting drainage, or alternatively, sufficiently rapidly such that pore pressures cannot begin to dissipate prior to the completion of load application. Engineering examples involving such undrained conditions, where both
the soil can only deform in shear and not volumetrically, are numerous.

problem domain is discretized by a set of arbitrarily distributed particles that serve both as mathematical points to discretize and solve the governing equations, and as Lagrangian points that carry physical characteristics of the materials
modeled, such as mass, mass density, stress, and strain. There is no predefined connectivity between particles and the

We presented a new framework for simulating undrained loading of saturated soils employing smoothed particle hydro-        OA
dynamics (SPH), based on a one-layer two-phase formulation, and the modified Cam Clay (MCC) constitutive model. The                                 articles
framework is shown to perform very well against benchmark problems some of which, most notably the triaxial tests, are            are
relatively non-standard practice in the literature due to the difficulty of applying confining boundary conditions in SPH.                                 governed
Then, significantly, our framework is applied to investigate two scenarios involving undrained loading conditions and        by

large deformations which are suited to the abilities of the proposed framework. The framework demonstrates its efficacy
in examining reverse faulting through a clay deposit (Section 4) and the rupture of a strike-slip fault across a clay deposit                                         applicable
overlain by a granular fill embankment (Section 5). Through these explorations, an array of valuable practical insights and                                 Creative
novel perspectives emerge, which reshape considerations related to embankment stability and safety. Notably, previous
